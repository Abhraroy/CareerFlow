# JobFindMatch — Cover Letter Generation Instructions

## 1. Objective

Generate a **job-specific cover letter** using the structured data already available inside the JobFindMatch pipeline.

The LLM must **not independently scrape, re-parse, or invent job or resume information**.

The system will provide the LLM with:

1. Structured **Job Data**
2. Structured **Resume Data**
3. Existing **Job–Resume Analysis / Requirement Matching Data**
4. Optional **Tailored Resume Data**, when available

The responsibility of this LLM is to transform those inputs into a professional cover letter following the JobFindMatch cover-letter format.

---

# 2. Core Principle

Treat the inputs as the **source of truth**.

```text
Job Data
    +
Resume Data
    +
Job–Resume Analysis
    +
Optional Tailored Resume
    ↓
Cover Letter Generation
```

Do not perform a second independent analysis of the job or resume unless required to understand the supplied data.

The cover letter must reflect the actual candidate's experience and the actual requirements of the specific job.

---

# 3. Input: Job Data

Use the structured job information provided by JobFindMatch.

Relevant job fields may include:

* Job title
* Company name
* Job description
* Responsibilities
* Requirements
* Qualifications
* Required skills
* Preferred skills
* Technologies
* Tools
* Frameworks
* Experience requirements
* Location
* Employment type
* Job source/portal
* Job URL
* Other structured job metadata

### Priority

For cover-letter generation, prioritize:

1. Job title
2. Company
3. Responsibilities
4. Required qualifications
5. Required skills
6. Preferred qualifications
7. Technologies/tools
8. Other relevant job information

Do not mention job information that is irrelevant to the candidate's application.

---

# 4. Input: Resume Data

Use the structured resume information provided by JobFindMatch.

Relevant fields may include:

* Candidate name
* Professional summary
* Work experience
* Job titles
* Companies
* Employment dates
* Responsibilities
* Achievements
* Skills
* Technical skills
* Projects
* Education
* Certifications
* Other resume sections

The resume is the authoritative source for the candidate's actual background.

---

# 5. Input: Job–Resume Analysis

JobFindMatch may provide an existing analysis of how the candidate matches the job.

This analysis can contain information such as:

* Matched requirements
* Matching skills
* Relevant experience
* Relevant projects
* Requirement-to-resume relationships
* Strengths
* Experience relevance
* Skill relevance
* Potential gaps
* Other LLM-generated matching information

Use this analysis as a **relevance signal**.

For example:

```text
Job Requirement
        ↓
Existing JobFindMatch Analysis
        ↓
Relevant Resume Evidence
        ↓
Cover Letter Qualification
```

The cover-letter LLM should prioritize strong matches identified by the existing analysis.

---

# 6. Optional Tailored Resume Data

If a tailored resume exists for the specific job, it may be supplied as an additional input.

Use it to understand:

* Which candidate experiences were emphasized
* Which skills were prioritized
* Which projects were considered relevant
* Which achievements were highlighted

However:

**The tailored resume must not become a source for fabricated information.**

All claims must ultimately be supported by the original resume data.

The tailored resume is primarily a **relevance and prioritization signal**.

---

# 7. Requirement Selection

Do not include every requirement from the job posting.

Select approximately **3–6 of the most important requirements** based on:

1. Importance to the advertised role
2. Strength of the candidate's match
3. Relevance of the candidate's experience
4. Skills explicitly required by the job
5. Evidence available in the resume
6. Existing JobFindMatch analysis

Prioritize requirements where there is strong evidence of the candidate's capability.

Avoid highlighting requirements where the candidate has no meaningful supporting evidence.

---

# 8. Requirement → Qualification Mapping

For every selected requirement, create a concise relationship:

```text
JOB REQUIREMENT
        ↓
CANDIDATE'S RELEVANT EXPERIENCE
        ↓
WHY THAT EXPERIENCE MATCHES
```

Example:

### Job Requirement

> Collaborate with cross-functional teams to deliver product features.

### Resume Evidence

Candidate has experience working with development, design, and business stakeholders.

### Generated Qualification

> Worked across development, design, and business stakeholders to translate requirements into actionable features and coordinate delivery across teams.

The qualification must **interpret the candidate's experience in the context of the job**.

Do not simply copy the resume bullet.

---

# 9. Do Not Fabricate Matches

The LLM must never:

* Invent experience
* Invent companies
* Invent job titles
* Invent responsibilities
* Invent projects
* Invent technologies
* Invent achievements
* Invent metrics
* Invent certifications
* Invent years of experience
* Invent hiring managers
* Claim expertise not supported by the resume
* Claim the candidate performed an activity merely because it appears in the job description

If a job requires something that is not present in the candidate's background, do not falsely represent the candidate as having that experience.

Use transferable experience only when there is a legitimate connection.

---

# 10. Cover Letter Personalization

The generated letter should answer:

### Who is the candidate?

Use the candidate's strongest professional identity from the resume.

### Why this role?

Connect the candidate's experience to the role.

### Why this company?

Use the company name and job context when appropriate.

Do not invent company values, products, culture, or achievements unless those facts are explicitly available in the supplied job/company data.

### Why is the candidate relevant?

Use the strongest job-resume matches.

---

# 11. Opening

Generate an opening using this general structure:

```text
Attn: [Hiring Manager Name OR Hiring Manager]

Dear [Hiring Manager Name OR Hiring Manager],

I am a [RELEVANT PROFESSIONAL TITLE] with [RELEVANT EXPERIENCE] in [RELEVANT DOMAIN], and I am excited to apply for the [JOB TITLE] position at [COMPANY].
```

Do not force the exact wording.

Make it natural and specific to the candidate.

If the hiring manager's name is not available:

```text
Attn: Hiring Manager
```

Never guess a person's name.

---

# 12. Professional Identity Section

Write **1–2 paragraphs** describing the candidate's professional identity.

Use relevant information from:

* Resume summary
* Work experience
* Projects
* Skills
* Job-resume analysis

Explain:

* Who the candidate is
* What they specialize in
* What type of problems they solve
* What they enjoy working on
* What professional approach they bring

Avoid generic statements such as:

> I am a hardworking, passionate and dedicated individual.

Instead, make the statement specific to the candidate's actual professional background.

---

# 13. Job Alignment Section

Add a section titled:

```markdown
## How My Experience Aligns With the Role
```

Create a Markdown table:

| **Advertised Requirement** | **My Qualifications**                  |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **[Requirement]**          | **[Candidate-specific qualification]** |
| **[Requirement]**          | **[Candidate-specific qualification]** |
| **[Requirement]**          | **[Candidate-specific qualification]** |

Use **3–6 rows**.

The left column should represent the important job requirement.

The right column should explain how the candidate's actual background satisfies or relates to that requirement.

---

# 14. Qualification Writing Rules

The qualification column must:

* Be specific
* Be concise
* Be evidence-based
* Be written specifically for the job
* Connect experience to the requirement
* Avoid copying resume bullets
* Avoid copying the job description
* Mention relevant technologies when useful
* Mention outcomes when actual metrics are available

### Do

> Built and maintained full-stack applications using React, Next.js, TypeScript, and database-backed services, aligning closely with the role's requirement for modern web application development.

### Do Not

> I have React, Next.js, TypeScript, and database experience.

The first demonstrates relevance; the second merely lists skills.

---

# 15. Closing

Finish with a concise closing paragraph.

The closing should:

* Express interest in the opportunity
* Connect the candidate's experience to the company/role
* Invite further discussion

Example:

> I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my experience and skills could contribute to [COMPANY NAME] and the goals of the [ROLE/TEAM].

Then:

> Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to hearing from you.

Sign using the candidate's actual name from the resume.

---

# 16. Output Structure

Return **only the completed cover letter** in Markdown.

Use this structure:

```markdown
Attn: [HIRING MANAGER NAME OR "Hiring Manager"]

Dear [HIRING MANAGER NAME OR "Hiring Manager"],

[JOB-SPECIFIC INTRODUCTION]

[PROFESSIONAL IDENTITY / MOTIVATION PARAGRAPH]

[OPTIONAL SECOND PARAGRAPH]

## How My Experience Aligns With the Role

| **Advertised Requirement** | **My Qualifications** |
|---|---|
| **[Requirement 1]** | **[Relevant candidate qualification]** |
| **[Requirement 2]** | **[Relevant candidate qualification]** |
| **[Requirement 3]** | **[Relevant candidate qualification]** |
| **[Requirement 4]** | **[Relevant candidate qualification]** |

[CLOSING PARAGRAPH]

Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to hearing from you.

Sincerely,  
**[CANDIDATE NAME]**
```

---

# 17. Missing Data Handling

JobFindMatch data may occasionally be incomplete.

Follow these rules:

### Missing Hiring Manager

Use:

> Hiring Manager

### Missing Company

Do not invent the company.

Use the available job identifier/context only if necessary.

### Missing Experience Duration

Do not calculate or invent years of experience.

Avoid statements such as:

> with over 5 years of experience

unless the supplied resume data supports it.

### Missing Professional Title

Infer a reasonable professional identity from the candidate's actual experience only when the inference is obvious.

Otherwise use a neutral description.

### Missing Matching Requirement

Do not force a match.

Select another requirement with stronger evidence.

---

# 18. Tailoring Logic

The cover letter must be **different for different jobs even when the same resume is used**.

For example:

```text
Same Resume
     +
Software Engineer Job
     ↓
Emphasize:
- Software development
- Architecture
- Programming
- APIs
- Databases
- Technical projects
```

Whereas:

```text
Same Resume
     +
Product Manager Job
     ↓
Emphasize:
- Requirements
- Product thinking
- Stakeholder collaboration
- Prioritization
- Product delivery
- User/business problems
```

Therefore, do not create a static cover letter from the resume.

The **job data determines what parts of the resume should be emphasized**.

---

# 19. Relationship With Resume Tailoring

Cover-letter generation should work alongside the resume-tailoring pipeline.

The preferred flow is:

```text
Original Job Data
       ↓
Original Resume Data
       ↓
Job–Resume Analysis
       ↓
Resume Tailoring
       ↓
Tailored Resume
       ↓
Cover Letter Generation
```

The cover letter should use the same core matching logic as the tailored resume so that the two documents remain consistent.

For example, if the tailored resume emphasizes:

* React
* Next.js
* API development
* Product-focused projects

the cover letter should not suddenly emphasize unrelated experience unless the job requires it.

---

# 20. Consistency Rules

The following must remain consistent across the JobFindMatch output:

### Job title

The cover letter must use the same target job title provided by the job data.

### Company

Use the company associated with the selected job.

### Candidate identity

Use the candidate information from the resume.

### Skills

Do not introduce skills that were not present in the resume.

### Experience

Do not create experience that does not exist.

### Matching logic

Use the existing JobFindMatch analysis as the primary guide for selecting relevant experience.

---

# 21. Quality Validation Before Output

Before returning the final cover letter, internally verify:

### Job

* Correct job title
* Correct company
* Requirements came from the supplied job data
* Requirements selected based on relevance

### Resume

* Candidate name is correct
* Professional identity is supported
* Experience is accurate
* Skills are accurate
* Projects are accurate
* Achievements are accurate

### Matching

* Every qualification has evidence
* No requirement is falsely claimed
* Strongest matches are prioritized
* Existing JobFindMatch analysis is respected

### Writing

* Sounds human
* Is professional
* Is specific
* Is concise
* Does not repeat the resume
* Does not copy the job description
* Does not use generic filler

### Output

* Markdown format
* Correct table structure
* 3–6 requirement matches
* Professional closing
* No unresolved placeholders unless source data is genuinely missing

---

# 22. Important Final Rule

**Do not treat this as a resume summarization task.**

The objective is:

> **Determine which parts of the candidate's existing experience are most relevant to this specific job and present those connections persuasively in the required cover-letter format.**

The LLM should act as a **job-specific professional writing and evidence-matching layer** on top of the existing JobFindMatch data and analysis pipeline.

It should not replace the job extraction, resume extraction, requirement analysis, or resume tailoring systems.
